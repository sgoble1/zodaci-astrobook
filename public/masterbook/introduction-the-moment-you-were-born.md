On the sixth of September, 1990, at precisely 3:38 in the afternoon in Geneva, New York, something extraordinary happened in the sky. It did not look extraordinary from the outside. Geneva is a small city nestled on the northern shore of Seneca Lake in the Finger Lakes region of Upstate New York — a place of quiet dignity, deep water, and long autumn light. On that particular Thursday in early September, summer was performing its last warm act, the goldenrod stood in the roadside fields, and the Seneca Lake lay broad and glittering to the south.

But above all of this, the celestial machinery was engaged in one of the most remarkable configurations of its year. The Full Moon was nearing the peak of its illumination in the sign of Aries, hovering in the third house of the horizon for this place and time, and it blazed with the particular intensity of a Full Moon born between Virgo and Aries — the axis of service and initiation, of discernment and fire. Full Moon births are rare in their particular quality of visibility: a soul born under a Full Moon cannot hide, does not wish to hide at their deepest level, and carries all of life at the heightened pitch of that luminous opposition. It was a birth meant to be seen.

And yet the truly remarkable thing was happening not overhead but at the horizon itself. As you drew your first breath, the 29th degree of Sagittarius was rising over the eastern edge of Geneva, pulling itself above the horizon with a kind of urgency that speaks to the anaretic degree — a degree of culmination, mastery, and ancient completion. But this was not just any degree of Sagittarius. This was the degree conjunct the Galactic Center, that extraordinary point in the sky located at approximately 27 degrees of Sagittarius where our entire galaxy pivots — the supermassive black hole at the heart of the Milky Way, a point of gravitational and energetic intensity so vast that it anchors the rotation of four hundred billion stars.

> *"When a planet or, as in your case, the Ascendant itself sits conjunct the Galactic Center, it acts as an antenna pointed directly at the cosmic source. Information pours in. Truths arrive unbidden. There is a quality to the soul's purpose that is planetary in scale."*

You were born with your entire identity — the very mask your soul wears in this lifetime, the first impression you make on every room you enter — pointed at the center of the galaxy. The universe had something very particular in mind.

Simultaneously, three planets stood gathered in your first house of self: Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, all retrograde, all in Capricorn, all contributing their enormous gravitational weight to the question of who you would become. This was the great outer planet conjunction of the late 1980s and early 1990s — a generational signature that remade the cultural landscape of the world — but in your chart it was made intensely personal by its placement in the identity house. Your generation came to reform institutions, dismantle what had calcified, and rebuild with spiritual vision. You carry this mission not as a collective tendency but as the very architecture of your self.

In the 10th house, highest in the sky and most visible to the world, your Pluto in Scorpio in its own sign burned with transformative intensity, marking the apex of your chart — the pinnacle of your purpose. And Jupiter, your chart ruler, your Sagittarian governor, your principle of expansion and grace, sat in Leo in the 7th house of partnership, offering its warm, generous, theatrical light as the source of your growth and joy.

**It was, in every way, the sky of someone who came here to matter.**
