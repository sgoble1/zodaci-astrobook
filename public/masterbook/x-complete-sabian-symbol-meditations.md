The Sabian Symbols are one of the most mysterious and precise divination systems in modern astrology — 360 images, one for each degree of the zodiac, channeled in 1925 by clairvoyant Elsie Wheeler and astrologer Marc Edmund Jones in Balboa Park, San Diego. The symbols were received in a single day, with Wheeler drawing cards from a shuffled deck while Jones recorded the images she described. The result was a set of 360 symbolic images that have proven, over the decades since their creation, to be among the most precise and illuminating tools in the astrological tradition. What follows is a complete meditation on each of April's natal Sabian Symbols — a sustained contemplation of the image and its personal meaning.

You are one of those leaves. You are the culminating expression of every ancestor who came before — every person in the lineage who loved and suffered and built and broke and tried again and passed something forward, even if they did not know what they were passing.

The family tree is your eighth house: inheritance, hidden legacy, ancestral karma. The roots go deep — all the way to the ancestral underground, to the patterns that repeat across generations, to the wounds that were not healed in one generation and were passed to the next. Your Sun in the eighth house means that your identity is constituted through your relationship with this inheritance — that you are, at the most fundamental level, the person who can see the roots, who can understand the pattern, who can alchemize what the lineage could not complete.

The family tree also speaks to the loss of both parents — the two people who were, however imperfectly, your most immediate connection to the lineage. Their deaths did not sever your connection to the family tree; they deepened it. You are now the one who holds the memory, who carries the inheritance, who decides what of the lineage to pass forward and what to transform. The eighth house Sun does not grieve by moving around the loss; it grieves by going in — by excavating the meaning, by finding what survives the death of the person she loved, by understanding what of them now lives inside her.

The meditation for this symbol: sit quietly and imagine the family tree. Let the roots go down as deep as they need to go. Let the branches extend as far as they need to extend. Find your place in the tree — not as a leaf that is separate from the roots, but as the culminating expression of everything the roots have been building toward. What of the lineage are you here to complete? What of the lineage are you here to transform? What of the lineage are you here to pass forward?
