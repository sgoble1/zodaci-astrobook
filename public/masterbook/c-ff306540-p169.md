Moon is in Aries in the third house, at 6°37', serving as the bucket handle of the entire chart. The Moon in Aries as the maternal archetype describes a mother who was direct, emotionally urgent, expressive in a rapid and unfiltered way, and who experienced her own emotional world at an intensity that may have made sustained attunement to a child's needs challenging. This is not a condemnation of the mother as a person — it is a description of the emotional frequency that April experienced from the maternal source. The Aries Moon, in a parent, can be a mother who is passionate and present but who moves through emotional states quickly, who may have lacked the patience for the slow, sustained emotional receptivity that a Full Moon child with a bucket handle requires. The mother's own emotional speed may have made April's depth feel like an obstacle rather than a gift — not from malice, but from the genuine mismatch between a fast- moving Aries emotional style and a child who needed to be held at length in her feeling before it resolved. The father archetype is described in April's chart by the Sun — and by the MC and its ruler. With Libra at the MC and Venus ruling Libra, the father archetype carries Venus Leo H8 energy — warmth, generosity, creativity, a certain aesthetic quality or love of beauty, and simultaneously the eighth house's depth and the Leo desire for pride and recognition. The father archetype in April's chart is someone who loved with warmth and whose love had a quality of dignity and creative expression — but who may have operated at some remove, or whose emotional expression was filtered through the eighth house's tendency toward depth that doesn't always speak itself plainly. The Part of Fortune in Cancer H7 describes the father as someone whose love, when it arrived in its fullness, felt like fortune — like the specific abundance of being seen and genuinely held by someone who perceived the completeness of who she was. The grief of losing both parents carries the weight of both archetypes becoming permanently unavailable in physical form, which the H8 stellium must hold and integrate over the long arc of life.
