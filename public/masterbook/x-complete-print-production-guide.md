*Technical Specifications for the Physical Book*

✦ ◆ ★ ◆ ✦

The following complete print production guide provides technical specifications for the physical production of April — The Complete Master Astrological Compendium. These specifications are designed to ensure that the book's visual and tactile qualities honor the extraordinary content it contains — that the physical object is as carefully crafted as the astrological analysis it presents.

PAPER SPECIFICATIONS: Interior paper: cream-toned archival quality, ideally 100gsm uncoated; avoid bright white. The cream tone is essential — it provides the warm, antique quality that the Brett Helquist illustration style requires, and it creates the visual continuity between the text pages and the illustration pages. Cover/final full-page art: heavier stock, 200gsm minimum or mounted cover boards for case-bound construction. The heavier stock for the full-page illustrations (Illustrations 01 and 16) ensures that the illustrations can be fully appreciated without the text showing through from the reverse side.

BINDING SPECIFICATIONS: Binding: case-bound with sewn signatures preferred so half/full-page art opens flat without gutter loss. The sewn signature binding is essential for a book of this size and quality — it ensures that the book opens flat, that the illustrations are not lost in the gutter, and that the book can be read and returned to many times without the binding deteriorating. The case-bound construction provides the physical durability that a book designed to be returned to at the thresholds, at the crossroads, at the mornings after the nights that changed everything requires.

INK SPECIFICATIONS: Gold: Pantone 871 metallic spot ink recommended; CMYK approximation only as fallback (C:0 M:18 Y:55 K:25). The Pantone 871 metallic gold is essential — it provides the warm, luminous quality that the gold accents in the illustrations require. The CMYK approximation is a fallback only — it cannot fully replicate the metallic quality of the Pantone spot ink. Black: rich black for large dark coverage; 100K black for fine linework where appropriate. The distinction between rich black and 100K black is important for the illustration quality — the fine linework of the Brett Helquist style requires the precision of 100K black.

PROOFING SPECIFICATIONS: Proofing: verify gold plates, small line retention, title clearances, spine width, and edge bleed before press. The gold plates must be verified separately from the black plates — the metallic quality of the Pantone 871 can be affected by the printing process in ways that are not visible in digital proofs. The small line retention is critical for the Brett Helquist illustration style — the fine crosshatching must be retained at full resolution. The title clearances must be verified for Illustration 01 (the cover) — the title text must be clearly legible against the dense starfield background.

COVER SPECIFICATIONS: The cover must use Illustration 01 — The Threshold Between Worlds — as a full-bleed image. The figure must be in the lower two-thirds of the cover. The title must occupy the upper third. The Galactic Center spiral must be the dominant gold accent. The corners must be vignetted and the sky must be densely hatched. The spine must use the title set vertically or horizontally per binding, with a small gold Galactic Center glyph centered. The back cover must use a cream/ink field with a restrained border, a small gold spiral or zodiac wheel mark, and an optional short synopsis block and production credit.

FINAL ART QA CHECKLIST: Every illustration has one dominant specified light source and maintains high-contrast crosshatching. April's posture remains composed, forward-capable, and never defeated. Gold appears only on sacred/significant objects: Galactic Center, candles, labels, glyphs, crown, dawn, selected borders. All dimensions and document placements match the placement map. Illustrations 01 and 16 form a deliberate pair: dark threshold vs dawn forward motion; final image reverses shadows behind her. No AI output should be treated as final art unless redrawn/refined to match deliberate pen-and-ink linework.
