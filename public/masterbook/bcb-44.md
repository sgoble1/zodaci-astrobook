# Appendix B — Glossary of Astrological Terms

Ascendant (Rising Sign): The zodiac sign that was rising on the eastern horizon at the exact moment of birth. Describes the persona, physical appearance, and instinctive approach to life. The first house cusp.

Midheaven (MC): The highest point in the natal chart, representing the cusp of the 10th house. Describes public reputation, career, and the mark one leaves in the world.

Natal Chart: A map of the sky at the exact moment, date, and location of a person’s birth. Also called a birth chart or horoscope. The foundational document of personal astrology.

Aspect: An angular relationship between two planets or points in the natal chart, measured in degrees. Key aspects include the conjunction (0°), sextile (60°), square (90°), trine (120°), and opposition (180°). Each aspect describes a quality of relationship between the planetary energies involved.

Transit: The current position of a planet in the sky as it moves through the zodiac, and the angle it forms with the planets and points in the natal chart. Transits describe the timing and quality of current astrological weather for an individual.

Progression: A predictive technique in which the natal chart is symbolically moved forward in time to describe the soul’s internal evolution. Secondary progressions use the equivalence of one day after birth equaling one year of life.

Dispositor: The planet that rules the sign another planet occupies. For example, Mars is the dispositor of any planet in Aries because Mars rules Aries. Dispositors reveal how planetary energies are governed and routed through the chart.

Anaretic Degree: The 29th degree of any zodiac sign — the last degree before a planet or point moves into the next sign. Associated with urgency, culmination, crisis, and the completion of a long karmic arc in that sign’s themes.

Galactic Center: The rotational center of the Milky Way galaxy, located at approximately 27° Sagittarius in the tropical zodiac. Astrologically associated with cosmic transmission, universal truth, and a broader-than-personal orientation in any planet or point conjunct it.

Sabian Symbol: One of 360 symbolic images — one for each degree of the zodiac — channeled by astrologer Marc Edmund Jones and psychic Elsie Wheeler in 1925. Sabian Symbols provide an intuitive and often remarkably precise symbolic portrait of any planetary placement at a specific degree.

Profection: An ancient Hellenistic timing technique in which the chart’s active house moves forward one house per year of life, activating that house’s planetary ruler as the “Lord of the Year” for that twelve-month period.

Solar Return: The chart cast for the exact moment the Sun returns to its natal degree and minute each year, approximately on the birthday. Describes the themes, opportunities, and challenges of the coming year.

Retrograde: The apparent backward motion of a planet as seen from Earth, caused by the relative orbital speeds of Earth and the planet in question. Natally, retrograde planets describe energies that are more deeply internalized; in transit, retrograde periods invite review, revision, and inward processing.

Vertex: A mathematical point in the chart where the ecliptic intersects the prime vertical in the western hemisphere. Often called the “electric Ascendant,” it is associated with fated, cosmically arranged encounters and relationships.

Part of Fortune: An ancient Arabic Lot calculated from the positions of the Sun, Moon, and Ascendant. Describes the conditions under which an individual experiences their greatest worldly joy, abundance, and flourishing.

Black Moon Lilith: The lunar apogee — the point in the Moon’s orbit farthest from Earth. In the natal chart, it describes the wild, undomesticated, fiercely authentic dimension of the self that refuses to be tamed or made palatable for social convenience.

Chiron: A centaur body orbiting between Saturn and Uranus, discovered in 1977. In the natal chart, Chiron marks the place of the deepest wound AND the greatest healing gift — the mythological wounded healer who cannot heal his own wound but heals others from the wisdom of it.

Fixed Stars: The background stars of the night sky, fixed in position relative to each other, against which planetary positions are measured. Certain fixed stars carry significant astrological influence when they conjunct a planet or angle in the natal chart.

Nodes: The North Node and South Node of the Moon — mathematical points marking where the Moon’s orbital path crosses the ecliptic. The South Node describes past patterns; the North Node describes the soul’s intended direction of growth in this lifetime.

Stellium: A grouping of three or more planets in the same sign or house, creating a concentrated emphasis of that sign or house’s themes in the natal chart.

Final Dispositor: The planet at the end of a chart’s dispositor chain — the planet in its own domicile that receives the dispositor chain of every other planet in the chart without routing to any other planet. The integrating master intelligence of the entire chart.

Bucket Chart: A chart shape in which the majority of planets cluster on one side of the chart while a single planet (the “handle”) stands alone on the opposite side. The handle planet becomes the chart’s primary focal point, through which all other planetary energies must be expressed.

Decan: A division of each zodiac sign into three sections of ten degrees each. The first, second, and third decans of each sign carry a planetary sub-ruler that adds nuance to any planet or point falling within that ten-degree range.

Dwad: A further subdivision of each zodiac sign into twelve sections of two and a half degrees each, with each dwad corresponding to one of the twelve signs. The finest gradation of zodiac sub-signature available in traditional astrological technique.

Solar Arc: A predictive technique in which every point in the natal chart is moved forward by the same arc traveled by the progressed Sun from birth to the current age. Produces a relocated chart that describes the current life chapter’s major themes and activations.

Gene Keys: A system developed by Richard Rudd synthesizing the I Ching, Human Design, and traditional astrology into a language of genetic and spiritual evolution. Each individual carries a primary Shadow, Gift, and Siddhi (highest expression) based on their chart configuration.

Kua Number: A number from 1 to 9 calculated from the birth year and gender in classical Chinese Feng Shui, determining an individual’s element affiliation, auspicious directions, and the Feng Shui practices most beneficial for their energy type.

Feng Shui: The ancient Chinese practice of arranging physical spaces to harmonize the flow of qi (life energy) in ways that support the health, prosperity, relationships, and overall wellbeing of the inhabitants. The Bagua map and directional compass are primary Feng Shui tools.

Chakra: From the Sanskrit word for wheel or circle; one of the subtle energy centers described in Hindu and yogic traditions as spinning vortices of energy located along the spine and throughout the energy body, each governing specific dimensions of physical, psychological, and spiritual experience.

Shadow Work: The process — associated particularly with Jungian psychology but embraced across many spiritual and psychological traditions — of bringing unconscious patterns, beliefs, and emotional material into conscious awareness for the purpose of integration, healing, and the reclamation of energy formerly spent in suppression or projection.
