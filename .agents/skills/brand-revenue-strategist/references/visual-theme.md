# Visual Theme Review

## Inspect the product, not just its homepage

Sample public discovery, pricing, product/sample book, personal results, reading states and administration. Review logo alignment, type hierarchy, semantic color roles, contrast, graphics, spacing, density and consistency across available/locked/loading/failed states. Inspect current tokens and rendered pages before prescribing a theme.

Preserve the latest user-approved identity and any protected edition. Use Experience Architect's active workflow for journey and navigation analysis rather than duplicating it. Frame this lens around brand expression, legibility, perceived trust and product value.

## Define a distinctive direction

Tie the direction to the actual product and audience. Specify hierarchy, typography roles, palette roles, visual asset language and restrained motion. Prefer authentic product previews, meaningful chart diagrams and consistent subject-specific illustrations over generic decoration. Avoid interchangeable card grids, gradient blobs, fake dashboards and ornamental statistics; do not remove approved glass effects merely because they are fashionable.

Recommend shared semantic tokens and existing controls rather than page-by-page hardcoded styling. Preserve accessibility, reduced motion, stable dimensions and mobile readability. Do not sacrifice content access or page speed for cinematic effects.

## Evidence and acceptance

For each change show the affected state, the comprehension/trust problem and its smallest remedy. Validate material changes in desktop and mobile views, keyboard navigation and long-content/error states. Measure task completion, activation or purchase progression where available; aesthetic preference alone does not establish conversion uplift. Describe untested directions as hypotheses, and cite dated sources for claims about contemporary trends.
